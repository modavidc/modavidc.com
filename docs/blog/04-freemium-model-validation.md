## The Freemium Dilemma

Freemium can go two ways:
1. **Too generous** - Free tier so good that users never upgrade (Spotify model fails for B2B)
2. **Too restrictive** - Free tier so crippled that no one tries it (nobody converts from nothing)

For Grupero, the question was: what's the natural friction point for WhatsApp group analysis?

## Why 3 Analyses?

We chose a **3-analysis limit** for free users. Here's the logic:

1. **First analysis** - "Does this app actually work with my group?"
2. **Second analysis** - "Can I compare two exports, see if trends are real?"
3. **Third analysis** - "Should I upgrade or just delete the group?"

By analysis 3, a free user has full context. They know:
- ✓ Parsing works (no corrupt files)
- ✓ Metrics are useful (not garbage)
- ✓ UI is functional (no crashes on their data)

If they want a 4th analysis, the decision is clear: **"This is worth $4.99/month to me"** or **"Not for me."**

## The Conversion Funnel

```
100 downloads
  ↓
60 try first analysis (40% drop-off: too slow to parse, confusing UI)
  ↓
35 save a second analysis (42% drop-off: no interest in trend data)
  ↓
20 hit 3-analysis limit (43% drop-off: casual users, not pain point)
  ↓
8 upgrade to Pro ($4.99/mo = $60/year)
  ↓
3 stay subscribed after 6 months (62% churn, healthy for SaaS)
```

**Effective conversion rate: 5% of downloaders → paid subscribers.**

## Pricing Psychology

**$4.99/month** is chosen for three reasons:

1. **Sub-$5 is a different mental category** - Users don't send a PM to their manager about a $4.99/month tool. They just try it.
2. **$60/year positioning** - Annual pricing ($49.99) feels like a "deal" (17% discount), drives commitment.
3. **"Worth my Spotify login" test** - If the app cost as much as one Spotify account, would I use it? Yes. Same value per unit time.

## Limitations as Features

The 3-analysis limit isn't a bug, it's a **conversion checkpoint**. Every limitation in the free tier should be one question:
- Can users meaningfully use the product? ✓
- Does the limitation block their workflow? ✓
- Would they gladly pay to remove it? ✓

If all three are yes, the limit drives conversion. If any is no, you've picked the wrong limit.

## What NOT to Limit

**Do NOT limit:**
- Real-time analysis quality (always deliver perfect parsing)
- Core insight visibility (show group health scores)
- Data fidelity (don't watermark exports)

**Do limit:**
- Quantity (3 analyses, not infinite)
- Advanced features (IA chat, cross-group comparison, CSV export)
- Pro workflows (bulk imports, automations, scheduled reports)

## Metrics That Matter

Track weekly:
- Free users hitting limit (conversion readiness)
- Users upgrading immediately vs. after 7 days (urgency)
- Churn rate in first month (product-market fit)
- Reason for deletion (free or paid) - from email/feedback

## Conclusion

Freemium works when:
- Free tier is **generous enough to prove value**
- Paid tier removes **real friction**, not just arbitrary limits
- Pricing is **impulsive** ($5, not $50)

3-analysis limit for Grupero hits all three. It's the right constraint for a personal productivity tool where users ask: "Will I actually use this regularly?" By analysis 3, they have their answer.
